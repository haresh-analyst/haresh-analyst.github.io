1How are we doing, and where are we heading?
Overall touchless performance, the monthly trend, and a forecast for the months ahead.
What this shows
The share of invoices processed automatically ("touchless"), how it changed each month, and where it is likely to go next.
Why it matters
Touchless invoices need no manual work. A higher touchless rate means less effort, fewer delays, fewer errors.
Where the numbers come from
The Invoice Automation dataset. Each row is one invoice. We use the columns Document Create Date and Invoice Automation?.
How the forecast works
It continues the recent trend forward. You choose how many months ahead and which method. The shaded band shows the best case to worst case range, because the further ahead we look, the less certain it is.
So what
If the line and forecast are rising, automation is improving. If flat or falling, the team needs to act.

2Is our validation work actually helping?
Compares invoices before Team validation against invoices after, to prove the team's impact.
What this shows
Touchless % for invoices the Team has NOT yet validated ("Before") versus invoices the Team HAS validated ("After").
Why it matters
This is the proof the Team is making a difference. If "After" is higher, the team's validation work is paying off.
Where the numbers come from
The columns Validator (Before / After) and Status (Completed / Work in progress / Not started).
So what
A clear gap between After and Before is the number to show leadership. The progress line shows the gap growing over time.

3Which problems can the Team actually fix?
Splits the manual invoices into "Team can fix" and "other teams" so effort goes where it counts.
What this shows
Every manual invoice has a reason it was not automatic. We label each reason VMD (a vendor master data problem the Team can fix) or Non-VMD (belongs to AP or another team).
Why it matters
The Team should spend time only on what it can actually fix. This separates that work from the rest.
Where the labels come from
The official Celonis classification list provided by the manager. VMD covers vendor, bank, tax and currency master data issues.
So what
The purple slice is the Team's real workload. The biggest VMD reason is where to start.

Manual invoices: Team can fix vs cannot fix

Purple = VMD, the Team can fix these. Grey = Non-VMD, these belong to other teams.

5Which suppliers should the Team work on first?
A ready-made priority list. The top of the list gives the biggest improvement for the least effort.
What this shows
Suppliers ranked by a Focus Score from 0 to 100. Higher score = work on this supplier first.
How the score is built
Three things added together: how much manual work the supplier causes (40%), how much of it the Team can fix (35%), and how low their touchless % is (25%).
Why those weights
Manual volume matters most, so it gets the biggest share. VMD share is next because it is the work the Team actually controls. Low touchless rounds it out.
Why it matters
The team does not have to guess who to pick. Start at rank 1 and work down.
So what
Clearing the top of this list moves the overall touchless rate the fastest.
