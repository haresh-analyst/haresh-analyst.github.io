My work at SLB

SLB's supply chain moves essential items across the world so the right things reach the right place at the right time. Paying thousands of suppliers accurately, with minimal manual effort, is a critical part of that machine. Here is the part I worked on.

The starting point

Imagine thousands of supplier bills arriving every month. The best ones flow through the system automatically, no person needed. When I joined, only about 15 out of every 100 did that. The rest needed human hands, which is slow, repetitive, and expensive at scale.

A surprisingly common culprit? Tiny errors in supplier records, a bank detail here, a payment field there. One wrong entry and a bill falls out of the automatic flow.

My contribution

Working within the Supply Chain Data Performance Team, I extracted and connected data from SAP, Ariba, SharePoint and Excel, validated and corrected master records for more than 1,200 suppliers, and designed an operational Power BI dashboard covering over 40,000 invoices, one screen where management could see instantly whether automation was genuinely improving, month by month.

By the end of my internship, 36 out of every 100 invoices flowed through automatically, up from 15. My dashboard is how the company saw and tracked that change.

Root cause analysis

Invoices failed automation for many different reasons, and no team can fix everything at once. So I studied the failures, grouped them by cause, and ranked them by how many invoices each one blocked. That gave the team a clear answer to a simple question: if we can only fix three things this month, which three free up the most work?

Documentation & handover

A dashboard nobody understands dies the day its builder leaves. I wrote down every calculation, data source and business rule, and walked both business and technical stakeholders through it, so the solution could move into the company's enterprise reporting without being rebuilt from scratch.

I treat that as the finish line: my work should run perfectly well without me in the room.
